# Q404 literature and target check

**27 November 2026. High confidence on the source and statement, moderate confidence on prior-work coverage.** The recovered proof addresses the original almost-everywhere variance question for every remaining Ledermann equality pair. Its positive-measure ambiguity region disproves generic global identifiability. The positive-measure uniqueness region strengthens this by excluding almost-everywhere nonuniqueness. The bounded search found no earlier theorem with this full scope. The number of real admissible completions on the ambiguous region and the degree of the complex projection remain outside the classification.

## Original target

- Sturma, Kranzlmueller, Portakal and Drton, *Matching Criterion for Identifiability in Sparse Factor Analysis*, Psychometrika 91(2), 536–555 (2026), §2 defines generic sign-identifiability on an open real parameter space. Theorem 3.7 and opening of p. 542 explicitly leave equality at the Ledermann count as the residual open problem for full-ZUTA graphs. Example 3.8 discusses the six-variable, three-factor precedent. The arXiv latest version reviewed is v2, dated 9 January 2026. [Official journal](https://doi.org/10.1017/psy.2026.10079) · [Versioned §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02986v2#S3)
- Pinned Q404 asks for generic global identification of the strictly positive diagonal Ω in the unrestricted real covariance model Σ = ΛΛᵀ + Ω, equivalently generic uniqueness up to column signs in a full triangular pattern. It sets aside the cases (p,r) = (3,1) and (6,3), leaving (10,6), (15,10), (21,15) and the higher pairs. Writing m = p − r turns equality into p = m(m + 1)/2 with m ≥ 4. The loadings in the triangular statement are considered up to signs and their leading block is generically nonsingular. [Pinned Q404](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/mathematical-statistics.md#q404)

## Strongest nearby results

- Bekker and ten Berge, *Generic global identification in factor analysis*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 264, 255–263 (1997), prove almost-everywhere uniqueness **strictly below** the bound and give a closed form for the second candidate solution at six variables and three factors. Wilson and Worcester (1939) already analyze this small equality case. Formal solutions in a determinant or diagonal-completion calculation must still be tested for positive semidefiniteness of the Gram part and strict positivity of the residual diagonal. No extrapolation of the six-variable case is used here. [Bekker–ten Berge](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-3795(96)00363-1) · [Wilson–Worcester](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.25.2.73)
- Shapiro, *Rank-reducibility of a symmetric matrix and sampling theory of minimum trace factor analysis*, Psychometrika 47, 187–199 (1982), establishes the positive-measure threshold for rank reducibility by changing the diagonal. Shapiro’s 1985 paper and 1989 correction address generic local identification. The derivative criterion used here is also a case of the tangent-space condition for local uniqueness in Shapiro, Xie and Zhang’s matrix-completion paper, Theorem III.2. Local uniqueness allows more than one separated regular completion. [Shapiro 1982](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02296274) · [Shapiro 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(85)90038-2) · [Shapiro–Xie–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00047)
- Drton, Sturmfels and Sullivant, *Algebraic Factor Analysis: Tetrads, Pentads and Beyond*, Probab. Theory Related Fields 138, 463–493 (2007), study the polynomial constraints after eliminating the diagonal, including the dimension of the model closure. This algebraic projection forgets the signs of residual variances and the positive-semidefinite condition. A dimension count or complex generic degree does not imply that all, or any given number, of the complex completion branches are admissible over the reals. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0509390)
- Checked later work includes Hosszejni and Frühwirth-Schnatter’s *Cover it up! Bipartite graphs uncover identifiability in sparse factor analysis* (Journal of Multivariate Analysis). Its graph rules give sufficient sparse-model identification criteria. It does not settle the remaining dense equality family. [Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0047259X25001319)

## What the candidate settles

The proof constructs two different regular PSD rank-r matrices whose off-diagonals coincide and places the observed diagonal strictly above both. The two local inverses of the projection persist on a common open neighborhood. A separate kernel argument near a zero-noise boundary prevents additional admissible completions on another region with strictly positive noise. The precise outcome is an open ambiguity region and an open uniqueness region for every remaining equality pair. These conclusions concern real admissible decompositions, and continue to hold in the full triangular parametrization up to column signs. The local condition and the inverse-function method are standard. The combined global assertion is the point compared with prior work.

The bounded search used the official journal, latest arXiv and pinned catalog, the original rank-reducibility and local-identification sources, and exact terms for the p=10,r=6 case, global variance identification at equality and complex symmetric diagonal completion. No priority claim is made. The open-set proof does not classify all real fibers or count the solutions on the ambiguity region.
